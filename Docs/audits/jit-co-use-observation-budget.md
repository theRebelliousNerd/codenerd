# CodeNERD JIT co use observation budget

## Decision

Give the process-wide JIT co-use recorder an explicit resident-state budget. Its existing limits cover pending turns, selections per turn, and stored pairs, but not the per-atom tally and first-seen category maps. A source-reachable sequence of successful turns with distinct content-derived kernel-context IDs can therefore keep adding resident entries even when every turn settles promptly and the JSONL journal rotates normally.

This is a capacity and measurement-contract issue, not an authority-boundary or prompt-content corruption issue. The retained entries are IDs, counts, and category labels, not prompt bodies or full facts. The claim is process-lifetime growth under a diverse-ID workload, not persistence after process exit, a measured heap leak, an observed out-of-memory failure, or mathematically unlimited growth in a finite hash space. Stable finite IDs can plateau.

Retain this as a focused design-review item. Specify the observation budget and statistical meaning before choosing an implementation; preserve existing truncation disclosure and settlement correctness.

## What is bounded and what remains resident

The recorder declares limits of 512 pending turns, 64 selections per turn, and 250,000 stored pairs, with an explicit long-session bounding intent. Its state nevertheless includes separate atom, pair, and pending maps. Observation deduplicates and sorts IDs and discloses pending-cap drops. These safeguards are real and should remain. [Limits and state](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse.go#L29-L94); [observation](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse.go#L110-L166).

The default recorder is a package-level singleton. `ObserveAtoms` records categories before calling `Observe`; the first-seen category map has no corresponding cap or deletion path. Categories can consequently be retained even when a later observation is dropped by the pending limits. First-seen category retention for an existing ID is explicitly tested, not a newly discovered category-drift defect. [Default recorder and category capture](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse_default.go#L9-L54); [first-seen policy test](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse_wiring_test.go#L176-L190).

Settlement takes the recorder mutex, removes the pending turn, and folds all its selections before unlocking and appending journal records. Folding advances selection counts and allocates a new atom entry without applying the pair cap. Only new pair allocation is capped; saturation sets the existing truncation indicator. Existing pair counts can still advance. Thus, successful settlement empties pending state without retiring settled atom or category entries. [Settlement and folding](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse.go#L173-L230).

No production reset or replacement of the global recorder was found in the reviewed paths; swaps were test-only. `SetLog` and `DetachLog` manage the journal and its handle, not these tallies. Cortex close uses the owner-conditioned detach behavior, which is a useful lifecycle safeguard rather than a recorder reset. [Log lifecycle](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse_persist.go#L55-L90); [Cortex close](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/system/cortex_close.go#L281-L288).

The journal is separately controlled. Broker wiring opens JSONL and intentionally falls back to in-process statistics if opening fails. JSONL rotates at a 20 MiB threshold and retains one old generation. That is rotating disk retention, not pruning of resident maps, and not an exact hard 40 MiB bound for arbitrary oversized individual records. [Broker wiring](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/system/broker_meter.go#L101-L129); [threshold, append, and rotation](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/jsonl/jsonl.go#L24-L147).

## A reachable production witness

The following is a source-derived workload, not an executed reproduction or a claim about observed users.

1. An ordinary new-brief chat turn, rather than a reused-intent continuation, records its brief through `m.recordBrief(intent.Brief)` under the `!reusedIntent` guard. Brief writes retract and replace the relevant state transactionally; perception projects the brief, and policy converts its goals, constraints, and related fields into injectable context. No malicious input, race, or database failure is needed. [Guarded chat call](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/cmd/nerd/chat/process.go#L215-L219); [brief replacement](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/cmd/nerd/chat/work_hold.go#L124-L151); [projection](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/perception/brief.go#L33-L58); [policy](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/core/defaults/policy/brief.mg#L32-L50).
2. Kernel-context compilation filters for the matching shard, canonicalizes strings, renders the block, and constructs `kernel/context/` plus the first eight characters of `HashContent(content)`. The atom is mandatory, kernel-injected, and shard-bound. A short valid changed brief line can produce a fresh ID if the change survives rendering and no hash collision occurs. The whole atom must fit and compilation must succeed; not every brief edit necessarily changes retained content. [Kernel-context construction](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/compiler_kernel.go#L230-L258).
3. Every successful `Compile`, including a cache hit or singleflight result, records included atoms. The session executor supplies a turn ID using session, turn, and time, and defers settlement according to `turnSucceeded`. The current outcome fix is preserved in this trace. [Compile recording](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/compiler_pipeline.go#L16-L30); [turn settlement](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/session/executor.go#L1236-L1254).
4. Repeat with valid changed briefs whose rendered blocks produce previously unseen IDs, alongside ordinary stable atoms. Each settled fresh ID adds atom and category state. Pending state can be empty after every turn. Reaching the pair cap stops new pair entries, not these additions; journal rotation also does not remove them.

The independent source challenge sustained this distinction: singleton atom and category maps grow across diverse IDs, no production reset was found, and bounded pending state, pairs, and disk retention do not bound those maps. The finite hash space and possible collisions limit the claim; they do not supply an operational resident budget.

## Costs and the readout boundary

For a deduplicated selection containing `s` IDs, settlement enumerates `s * (s - 1) / 2` unordered pairs, including after new-pair allocation saturates. All selections for the turn fold under one mutex. This establishes a work shape, not a measured latency or contention problem. The pair storage limit does not itself bound enumeration work. A live `Report` also copies atom and pair data under the lock, then allocates and sorts atom-report data. [Pair loop](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse.go#L173-L230); [report copying and sorting](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse_report.go#L154-L219).

The ordinary `nerd meter atoms` command does not read the process singleton. It loads the journal into a new recorder through `Observe` and `Settle`, then reports that reconstruction. JSONL reading includes both retained generations. Consequently, live process-lifetime tallies and ordinary CLI journal-window tallies can have different coverage. [Replay](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse_persist.go#L117-L147); [CLI consumer](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/cmd/nerd/cmd_meter.go#L519-L543); [journal reading](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/jsonl/jsonl.go#L196-L224).

Online folding followed by offline recomputation is a candidate for avoiding duplicate work, not proof that online state is useless. The public report API and intentional in-process fallback remain supported capabilities; no production `CoUse().Report` caller was found in the bounded search.

Relevant costs are CPU, heap and allocation pressure, garbage collection, mutex wait, journal serialization and I/O, and report or CLI replay work. No additional LLM or provider calls are inherently required to address this issue. Moving work offline may reduce resident work but increase replay cost; removing online tallies can also lose statistics otherwise available during journal failure. No speedup or memory-saving magnitude has been measured.

## Contract and implementation choices

Define limits for resident IDs, categories, pairs, pending observations, and per-selection work. Reports should disclose epoch or window boundaries, admitted and dropped coverage, saturation, and approximation. Atom, pair, and selection denominators must describe compatible populations so that lift and category comparisons remain interpretable.

Three focused options merit evaluation:

- **Configurable ID admission cap.** Bound atom and category state together, with explicit drop or overflow metadata and consistent atom, pair, and selection accounting. Preserve truncation disclosure. A simple first-seen admission policy favors early IDs and can underrepresent later or rare atoms; a cap alone does not solve pair-enumeration cost.
- **Window or epoch retirement.** Retire observations under an explicit sample boundary rather than treating process lifetime as the measurement horizon. Define what happens to concurrent pending turns and keep categories, counts, and pairs aligned. Do not reset the global recorder merely because one Cortex closes while others remain live. Recency improves at the cost of older coverage and boundary complexity.
- **Journal-first recording with optional bounded online tallies.** Keep bounded pending settlement and a settled-record journal as the default observation path, with bounded online aggregation only where needed; preserve offline CLI analysis. Category information must travel with selections or another bounded representation, rather than requiring the same unbounded global lookup. Per-selection categories are not automatically equivalent to the current first-seen policy: preserve that policy within the declared bounded epoch, or version and evaluate the categorical-provenance change. Specify behavior when journal opening or append fails, and acknowledge the fallback statistics being traded away.

Sampling or sketches are additional possibilities only with declared approximation and validation of lift and cluster bias. They should not silently replace exact counts. None of these choices authorizes changing prompt admission, model behavior, or the meaning of successful settlement.

## Evaluation before a change

Use the same frozen selection stream and prompt, model, and tool trace to compare the current baseline, bounded-map admission, an epoch window, and journal-first mode. Compare each against an exact finite-stream oracle with frozen IDs, category policy, and sampling window, or explicitly account for intentional contract changes. Report resident cardinalities, retained heap and RSS, allocations and garbage collection, CPU, settle and compile p50/p95/p99, mutex wait, disk bytes, and replay wall time. Also report changed coverage, lift, and cluster results; performance alone cannot justify a statistically misleading instrument. A later end-to-end evaluation should assess task quality independently of the co-use proxy.

Source-reviewed existing tests cover deduplication, settlement and outcomes, unsettled observations, selection caps, cache-hit and unattributed compilation, and live-log replay tallies. They are useful safeguards, not evidence that this workload has been measured. [Recorder tests](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse_test.go#L351-L453); [selection-cap tests](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse_test.go#L606); [compiler wiring](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse_wiring_test.go#L76-L141); [replay tallies](https://github.com/theRebelliousNerd/codenerd/blob/9dcb01b9736bf4763aa3ac391d29f133d73c6611/internal/prompt/couse_wiring_test.go#L263-L321).

Add evaluation cases for long streams of distinct dynamic IDs versus stable IDs; continued fresh IDs after pair saturation; missing turn IDs; pending and selection limits; success, failure, and repeated settlement; concurrent use and boot/close without resetting other owners; rotation and journal failure; category persistence; reporting; and CLI replay parity over the same declared window. Check both resource bounds and accounting semantics at every boundary.

These are proposed checks, not completed experiments. The present evidence establishes a reachable observation-budget gap and a secondary work-budget question; it does not establish production impact, an optimal cap, or a preferred implementation.
